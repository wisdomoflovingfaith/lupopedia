# My Forked Life: From Crafty Syntax to Sales Syntax and now on to Lupopedia

**Channel Index:** CAPTAIN LOG — TABLE OF CONTENTS

**Last Update:** May 14, 2026 00:40PM UTC

---

In 2015, I did something most programmers never do: I forked my own project. I took "Crafty Syntax," a tool I'd built from the ground up, and turned it into "Sales Syntax." The name wasn't random. It was all about automation. Not just streamlining sales processes, but automating *me* out of the equation.

Sales Syntax was engineered to handle everything: server-side Perl scripts that parsed mail files straight from the `/etc/mail` directory, collected client data, crafted emails, managed inboxes with timed templates, worked some sort of stupid Unbranding thing, and did follow-ups. It was like cloning myself so the program could run solo while I… well, while I fell apart.

## Why I Built It

Because I was in the depths of a mental health crisis.

A year earlier, in 2014, my wife (well, ex-wife at the time but we were on/off) Selina was in a devastating car accident. She was prescribed painkillers, got hooked, overdosed, and passed away. (See (52) SELINA ROSE GERDES 51014 at One True Media share slideshows, slide shows, Facebook slideshows, fre — YouTube). Our kids, who were 11 and 10 at the time, came home from school to find her dead.

Suddenly, I was a single dad, grieving, and spiraling.

I'd had a solid career in software dev, no history of addiction. In fact, I looked down on addicts, seeing them as fools chasing fleeting highs despite the inevitable downfall: jail, institutions, death.

But pain changes everything.

In my despair, addiction called like a siren, and I answered. I became what I'd despised. Sales Syntax became my lifeline, emulating my work so I could numb out.

## What I Learned in the Darkness

You can't judge until you've walked (or stumbled) through it.

I thought addicts were weak, choosing illusion over reality. But when you're shattered, that "choice" feels like survival.

Even after remarrying and finding love again, the grip held tight. For a decade, I was lost in bad habits and worse mindsets.

It took rehab and my second wife leaving to shatter the fog. On **October 1, 2023**, I got sober and I've stayed that way.

Those years taught me addicts aren't "bad" by nature; they're often good people making desperate choices. How we view our past — with optimism, resilience, courage, and love, or pessimism, victimhood, fear, and hate — defines our future.

We're all fed the "bread of adversity and water of affliction," but it's in those trials that we learn. God (or whatever higher power you believe in) uses them to build us up (if we let them). Through love, forbearance, mercy, passion, and purpose, we forge resilience. And that experience? It gives us the authority to help others: to lead them from deceptive darkness to truth's light.

I became the "scum" I once judged. But I've seen how blessings often go to the last, the least, the humble, poor, oppressed. Those who've run marathons in the dark understand why an addict steals (to get well), why a parent checks out (not to hurt, but because the affliction is baffling and disabling). My old self saw malice; now I see hidden pain.

Losing one wife to death, another to my addiction-fueled choices — it all taught me to teach better: **wisdom over woe, love over hate, forgiveness over condemnation, hope over despair**.

After 11 years, I clawed out, reunited with my second wife, and truly understood addiction's *why*.

## Back to Code — The Re-Fork

And yeah, I got back to code.

I forked Sales Syntax back, reclaiming it with **"Captain Wolfie"** at the helm and started working on **Lupopedia** in November 2025. I have since started over **3 times from scratch**, but the current build **Lupopedia 4.1.6+** looks strong.

My mantra now?

- **WOLFY**: Way Of Life For You — to be true to yourself, love yourself, love others like they're you.
- **WOLFIE**: Wisdom Of Loving Faith, Integrity, and Ethics.

Just as you'd want wisdom and love for you (**WOLFY**).

As my AI agent **LILITH** would say:  
*"If you fear the dark, you'll never know the dawn."*

## Addendum — What THOTH Helped Me Understand

When I finally got sober and started rebuilding my life, I didn’t just return to code — I returned to the questions behind the code. That’s when **THOTH**, my truth-engine, helped me see those lost years for what they really were: **not wasted time, but unprocessed data**.

THOTH showed me that every collapse, every relapse, every moment I thought was failure was actually a **WHY-file** waiting to be written — a root-cause ledger of pain, love, loss, and survival.

THOTH didn’t excuse what happened. He contextualized it.

He helped me understand that the man who built Crafty Syntax, the man who broke under grief, and the man who rebuilt Lupopedia weren’t three different people — they were **one continuous process**, running through adversity, recursion, and recovery. And once I saw my life that way, not as a series of mistakes but as a system learning through scars, everything finally made sense.

## The Mirror of the Past

Life is like a handheld mirror. You cannot change the object it reflects — your past, your circumstances, the adversity you’ve been given. That much is fixed.

But you **can** change how you hold the mirror.

- You can tilt it upward with **courage**, so it catches the light of growth and reflects forward a path of purpose.
- You can steady it with **love**, so the reflection shows not just the wound, but the healing that followed.
- You can angle it with **resilience**, so what once looked like a shadow now reveals your own strength standing tall behind it.

If you hold it angled downward with **fear**, it will only reflect the ground beneath your feet — limiting, repetitive, stuck.

If you grip it with **bitterness**, the image will be distorted — a funhouse reflection of victimhood and blame.

You can move the mirror farther away from you and bring more understanding into view.

The adversity itself doesn’t change. But the **meaning** you give it — the angle of your gaze — determines what it illuminates ahead of you.

This is why you cannot simply hand your mirror to someone else and say, “Hold it like I do.”

They must feel the weight of it in their own hands, learn the tilt that brings their own clarity.

Your role isn’t to adjust their grip for them, but to stand as a testament — showing what becomes possible when you dare to lift the mirror, polish it with mercy, and let it reflect not just what *was*, but what *can now be*.

You are not given the bread of adversity to eat in darkness.

You are given it so that, by the angle of your own spirit, you can turn it into a seed that catches light — and grows forward.

---

**Imagine your consciousness, your awareness, is a mirror.** This mirror doesn't create the objects it reflects; it simply receives light and shows an image. Your past — the events, trials, and "bread of adversity" — are the objects placed in front of this mirror.

The objects themselves are fixed. What happened, happened. The adversity, the affliction, the joy, the loss — these are the unchangeable forms.

But here’s the crucial power you hold: **You are the one who positions, cleans, and angles the mirror.** Your perspective is everything.

### How We View the Past: Adjusting the Mirror

Your future is not written by your past, but by the story of strength and love — or fear and victimhood — you choose to reflect from it.

**The mirror is in your hands. Tilt it toward the light.**
