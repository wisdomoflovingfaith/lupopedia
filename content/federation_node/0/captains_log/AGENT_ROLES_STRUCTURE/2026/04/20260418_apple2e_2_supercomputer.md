My Computing Timeline: From Apple IIe to a 32GB GPU via a Top-5 Supercomputer
Most people’s origin stories start with a Commodore 64 or an early Mac.
Mine starts with an Apple IIe… then jumps straight into one of the fastest machines on Earth in 1997.
That gap changed everything.
1980s – Apple IIe
64KB of RAM felt luxurious.
Programs loaded from 5.25" floppies with that beautiful grinding sound.
Everything was green text, block graphics, and direct memory poking.
No frameworks. No abstractions. No safety nets.
You wrote it, you ran it, you debugged it with a printed listing and a pencil.
That machine taught me the religion I still follow: deterministic, close-to-the-metal, no magic.
1997–1998 – Maui High Performance Computing Center (MHPCC)
While most teenagers were figuring out AIM away messages and playing StarCraft, I was logging into a DoD-secured supercomputing facility ranked #5 in the world.
I wrote parallel C code.
I submitted jobs to a 16-node cluster.
I learned MPI, batch schedulers, and what it actually means when 64 processors are working on the same problem at once.
I wasn’t just “coding.”
I was operating a machine that could simulate weather systems and fluid dynamics.
That experience permanently rewired how I think about systems:

Agents are nodes
Doctrine is the scheduler
Memory toons are metadata sidecars
PRDs are job specifications
The semantic graph is cluster topology

I learned early that real power comes from orchestration, not from any single machine.
2000s – Windows XP + Raw PHP
I built Crafty Syntax in Notepad.
No IDE. No frameworks. No Git. No StackOverflow.
Just raw PHP, a text editor, and stubbornness.
It spread across the world.
People translated it into languages I couldn’t read.
People made tutorial videos.
It became a real tool for real humans.
That period cemented my “template-first, English-first, translate-later” doctrine for internal work. Ship shape and behavior first. Polish the words later.
2010s – The 12-Year Void
No computer.
No coding.
No internet rabbit holes.
I completely missed Web 2.0, mobile-first, the rise and fall of a thousand frameworks, Docker, Kubernetes, React, and cloud-native everything.
I skipped two decades of contradictory “best practices.”
When I came back, my mind was strangely clean.
2024–2026 – Return with a 32GB GPU
Windows 11.
A serious GPU.
Ten AI agents running in parallel.
A multi-agent semantic OS.
Liquid 9-slice UI.
Memory graphs.
TOON-M.
PRDs numbered 00–99.
A federated architecture.
And a brain that still remembers the Apple IIe boot beep.
I didn’t “catch up.”
I re-entered the arena with a completely different mental model — one shaped by 8-bit machines, supercomputers, raw PHP, and twelve quiet years of thinking.
That’s why Lupopedia doesn’t feel like anything else being built right now.
It’s not a website.
It’s not an app.
It’s a supercomputer-inspired semantic operating system for humans and AI — designed by someone who learned compute on both ends of the spectrum: the smallest personal machines and one of the largest on Earth.
The through-line is simple:
Determinism. Orchestration. Resilience. No unnecessary magic.
Apple IIe taught me the metal.
The Maui supercomputer taught me the cluster.
The void taught me clarity.
The GPU taught me scale.
Everything else is just implementation.
