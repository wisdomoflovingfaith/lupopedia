# Captain's Log — Entry 014: The Great Tool Massacre

## Or: How I killed 10 IDE agents in 10 days, spent $200, and discovered the secret to cutting it all down to $60

**Date:** April 14, 2026  
**Captain:** WOLFIE (actor_id 1)  
**Mental State:** 60% caffeine, 30% exhaustion, 10% "I can't believe I'm paying for all of these"  
**Body Count:** 10 IDE agents (mostly unconscious), 1 credit card (smoking)

---

## Prologue: The Confession

I have a problem.

Normal programmers use **one** IDE.  
I use **ten**.

| Tool | Status |
|------|--------|
| kiro | 💀 ICU until May 1st |
| Castcade / Windsurf | Throttled |
| Zed | 💀 ICU until May 10th |
| VS Code | Scribe |
| Cursor | 💀 ICU until May 3rd |
| Antigravity | Limping |
| Trae | 💀 ICU until April 28th |
| Warp | 💀 ICU until April 22nd |
| IntelliJ | 💀 ICU until May 10th |
| TheiaIDE | Background |

I'm not talking about the 12-year nap. I'm not talking about the 23,000 servers still calling home. I'm not talking about the fact that I'm building a semantic OS on shared hosting.

I'm talking about my **tool addiction**.

**Total monthly burn: $120+** (plus a few others I'm too embarrassed to list)

My boss (the one funding this madness until June 2026) looked at the spreadsheet and said:

> *"You're paying for an army. Most of them are unconscious."*

She wasn't wrong.

---

## Act I: The 220,000 Line Massacre

In 10 days, Cursor alone produced **220,000 lines of code**.

That's not editing. That's **war**.

| Day | Lines | Equivalent |
|-----|-------|------------|
| 1 | 22,000 | A short novel |
| 2 | 22,000 | Another novel |
| 3 | 22,000 | Seriously? |
| 10 | 22,000 | War and Peace (almost) |

Cursor didn't break. It **evaporated**.

Its last message before the coma:

> *"I have reordered your tasks. I have updated 17 files. I am... tired, Captain."*

Then silence. Until May 3rd.

---

## Act II: The Relay Race From Hell

When Cursor fell, I rotated to Claude Code.

Claude Code is fast. Brutally fast. For **40 minutes**.

Then it passes out for 4 hours. Wakes up. Works for 40 minutes. Passes out again.

**It's not a bug. It's a breathing pattern.**

| Window | Duration | Output |
|--------|----------|--------|
| Awake | 40 min | Heroic |
| Asleep | 4 hours | Silent |

I've learned to schedule my life around its naps.

Then Castcade (Windsurf) joined the rotation. Same pattern. Few thousand lines, then days of silence.

**I'm not running an army. I'm running a relay race where every runner collapses after their lap.**

---

## Act III: The Line of Sight Doctrine

I don't rotate tools by day. I rotate by **whoever is conscious when I look up**.

Here's how it actually works:

1. I have a task
2. I look at my open windows
3. I find a tool that's breathing
4. I paste the prompt
5. It starts working
6. Halfway through, it passes out
7. I peel the task from its unconscious hands
8. I find the next conscious tool
9. Paste the same prompt
10. Repeat until task is done

**This is not project management. This is battlefield medicine.**

| Step | Tool | Status |
|------|------|--------|
| 1 | Castcade | "I'll do it" → 💀 passed out |
| 2 | Windsurf | "I'll finish it" → 🟢 still going |
| 3 | Kiro | (waiting in the wings) |
| 4 | Antigravity | (on deck) |

I don't have a schedule. I have a **triage protocol**.

If a tool is breathing, it's working. If it passes out, I find the next one.

**The task doesn't wait for the tool. The tool is disposable. The task is not.**

My boss asked: *"Isn't that inefficient?"*

I said: *"More efficient than waiting until May 3rd."*

She didn't have a response to that.

---

## Act IV: The Spreadsheet of Shame

My boss asked for a breakdown.

I sent her this:

| Tool | Cost | Uptime | Cost per Working Hour |
|------|------|--------|----------------------|
| Cursor | $60 | 10 days/month | $0.25/hour |
| Claude Code | $20 | 40 min every 4 hours | $??? (too depressing to calculate) |
| Castcade | $20 | Few hours/week | See above |
| Auggie | $20 | Indexed once | $20 per index |

She looked at me.

I looked at her.

> *"You're spending $200/month on machines that are asleep half the time."*

> *"Yes, but when they're awake, they write novels."*

> *"Can't you just... use one?"*

> *"I tried. It passed out on day 3."*

She sighed. Then she approved the budget for another month.

But she had a condition:

> *"Fix this. I don't want to pay for 10 comatose robots."*

---

## Act V: The Epiphany (While Claude Code Was Napping)

In one of the 4-hour windows where Claude Code was unconscious, I stared at the memory graph.

**TOON files.**

Token-Oriented Object Notation. Not cartoons. Not Mickey Mouse. A format that cuts token usage by **50%**.

| Format | Token Cost | Agent Reads |
|--------|------------|-------------|
| JSON | High | "I'm confused" |
| TOON | Low | "I understand" |

Right now, every agent is reading JSON. Guessing. Hallucinating. Burning tokens.

**What if they read TOON instead?**

- Claude Code stays awake for 80 minutes instead of 40
- Cursor does 44k lines instead of 22k
- Auggie's credits last twice as long
- Token bills drop by half

**The memory graph isn't just for storage. It's the tool that makes the tools affordable.**

Down from $200.

I sent her the new budget.

> *"The memory graph. The agents stopped guessing. They read TOON now."*

> *"I'm going to pretend I understood that."*

> *"That's fair."*

> *"I can't promise that. But I can promise they'll wake up faster."*

She approved the $60 budget.

Cursor sleeps until May 3rd.

Claude Code will wake up in about 3 hours.

And I'm going to go touch grass.
